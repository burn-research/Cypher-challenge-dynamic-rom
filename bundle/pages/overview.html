CYPHER 2026: data-driven reduced-order modeling of an externally forced laminar flame

Introduction

High-fidelity CFD simulations of reacting flows are accurate but far too expensive to run in real time. Reduced-order models (ROMs) aim to reproduce the dynamics of these systems at a fraction of the computational cost, which is essential for real-time control, monitoring, and digital twins of combustion systems.

Challenge description

This hackathon is dedicated to building a data-driven reduced-order model of a dynamical combustion system: a 2D laminar flame subject to a time-varying inlet velocity.

You are given two training simulations in which the inlet velocity follows a "sine sweep" (chirp) profile, ramping linearly in frequency from 1 Hz to 80 Hz and back to 1 Hz, at two different forcing amplitudes. Your model will then be evaluated on unseen forcing signals it has never encountered during training: step changes and single-frequency sine excitations, at amplitudes and frequencies different from the training set.

Given only the initial flow state and the future forcing signal (the inlet velocity profile is known in advance, exactly like a control input), your model must forecast the full spatio-temporal evolution of all 11 flow fields over the whole time horizon.

Submissions are evaluated in terms of forecasting accuracy (NRMSE), computational cost (training and inference time), and physical consistency of the predicted fields.

Working in teams

Participants work in teams of 3 to 4 people. The best-performing models in terms of accuracy, computational speed, and physical consistency will be rewarded.
